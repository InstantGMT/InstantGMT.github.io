One policy, in minutes

Endless choices.
Learned in 15 minutes.

An operator never follows a script: every few seconds the stream can branch into a new gait, a new style, a turn, a run. One InstantGMT policy tracks every branch, and it gets there fast. After 15 minutes on one GPU it falls on 1–3 of 198 held-out motions; in the same 15 minutes SuperMimic falls on 11–14 and PPO on 100–110. Speed

Same data. Same GPU.
A fraction of the time.

Same 22.6 h motion library, same RTX 5090, same evaluation. After 15 minutes, InstantGMT tracks more accurately than SuperMimic after 3 hours, and falls far less than PPO after 12.

15 minInstantGMT reaches 3.7 cm body error, below SuperMimic after 3 h (4.4 cm)

Paper, Table 1: 198 held-out motions on a hardware-calibrated G1 model, method-neutral evaluation; same 22.6 h library and the same RTX 5090 for every method. The checkpoints were written every 15 minutes, and the time points were fixed before the runs. Falls: the line is the mean over seeds (three, PPO two) and the whisker spans the best and worst seed. Body error: as reported in the paper (PPO seed 0). Trained with InstantGMT's randomization, SuperMimic reaches 3.3 cm after 3 h, close to InstantGMT's 3.1 cm. After 3 h, InstantGMT's advantages are smoother PD targets, robustness to pushes and its much faster early learning.

Method

One loop. Nothing wasted.

InstantGMT keeps SuperMimic's loop: a learned world model and analytic policy gradients through it. It changes how the loop runs, not what it learns.

01

An averaged policy

The policy we evaluate is an exponential moving average of the trained one. Less noise between checkpoints, earlier good checkpoints.

02

Muon

Orthogonalized updates for the hidden layers of the world model and the policy. Same learning-rate schedule.

03

No host syncs

Guards, metrics and timers stay on the GPU, so the CPU never waits for it inside an iteration.

04

One graph per step

The parallel-ankle closure no longer breaks the compiled world-model step. Each update replays as a CUDA graph.

05

Read each motion once

The reference windows of a rollout overlap. One gather per rollout from the host-side library replaces ten.

06

The same policy

Network, observations, actions and rewards are unchanged. The checkpoint replaces one trained the usual way, with nothing else to change.

In simulation

One policy, many skills.

The same InstantGMT policy, after 15 minutes of training, tracking reference motions in MuJoCo on the calibrated G1 model: the physics of the evaluation, rendered in Blender. Motions from 100STYLE (training set) and from our own VR teleoperation recordings; no falls in any clip.

A second humanoid

InstantGMT on a 1.6 m humanoid with 27 actuated joints and closed-loop parallel ankles: the policy drives the ankle motors, and the linkage is simulated in MuJoCo. This policy was trained longer than the G1 policies above. 100STYLE motions; no falls in any clip.

Deployment

From one GPU to the robot.

On hardware, with live VR teleoperation: the operator moves, the robot follows. Every clip is real time, uncut within the clip, with the operators' faces blurred. In the two-robot clips, both robots run InstantGMT.

Questions? Answers.

What is general motion tracking?

One policy that follows any reference motion, not a fixed set of clips. Here the reference streams live from a VR headset.

Does InstantGMT change what the policy learns?

No. Rewards, observations, network and robot interface are those of the GMT baseline it is compared with. InstantGMT changes the optimizer and how the training loop runs.

Is the comparison fair?

Same motion library, same GPU model, same evaluation. Details with the paper.

Has it run on hardware?

Yes. Every clip in Deployment is a real robot under live VR teleoperation, running an InstantGMT policy. Quantitative real-robot results come with the paper.
